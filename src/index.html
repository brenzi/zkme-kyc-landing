Kusama Vision applicant verification

Identity and residence verification for the Kusama Vision Program, via zkMe.

What zkMe verifies

  • your identity: a genuine government ID plus a live selfie, so you are its holder
  • your residence: an address document not older than 90 days

What the curators receive, and what they never see

The curators receive, tied only to your anonymous verification ID:

  • your verified country of residence (the country only, e.g. "Switzerland")
  • your country of citizenship (shown to curators in zkMe's dashboard)
  • pass/fail results: residence outside the embargoed countries, identity and liveness passed, and zkMe's own sanctions pre-check

The curators never see your name, date of birth, address, documents or selfie through zkMe; those stay with zkMe. Identity details the curators need for their own sanctions screening are provided separately in your Matrix room, not through this page.

Depending on your case, the curators may additionally need, always announced in your Matrix room and never enabled silently by this page:

  • for some countries, a stronger proof of residence: the curators send a letter with a code to your address and you post the code back;
  • a repeat of the sanctions screening before each payout;
  • your consent to zkMe selectively disclosing your verified legal name and date of birth to the curators for their manual sanctions screening.

Where your raw data ends up, and the long-term risk

Be clear about what you are agreeing to. Your ID photos, selfie and address document are uploaded to and processed on zkMe's servers; this is not an on-device-only system. To meet legal retention duties, zkMe then keeps your raw verification materials, encrypted, in its zkVault on public decentralized storage (IPFS). Decryption requires the cooperation of multiple key holders and is intended for cases such as a lawful order; no single party can decrypt alone. The linked zkMe documentation explains the mechanism and what is stored.

The blunt part: ciphertexts on a public network can be copied by anyone, today, and kept forever. zkVault's key encryption is elliptic-curve cryptography, which future quantum computers are expected to break. If that happens, archived copies of your passport data could become decryptable. Treat the confidentiality of what you upload as time-limited, not permanent. If this is not acceptable to you, say so in your Matrix room: a manual verification process without zkMe exists.

This page itself stores nothing, has no backend, and talks only to zkMe. Sanctions screening is done separately by the curators; nothing here replaces it.

How it works

  1. The curators assign every beneficial owner of a proposal a personal verification ID (a random code starting with kv) after the signed declaration in your Matrix room. Your link carries your ID; it links your verification to your application and contains no personal data.
  2. Each curator verifies applicants independently, so complete the verification once per curator, in two steps: an identity check (ID scan and selfie) and a residence check (a proof-of-address document not older than 90 days, e.g. a utility bill or bank statement). The first identity run takes a few minutes; later runs reuse your zkMe credential and take seconds. Do the identity step before the residence step.
  3. When every row shows ✓ verified, tell the curators in your Matrix room.

Each curator sends you a personal verification link in your Matrix room; this page cannot start a verification without one. Opening a link shows that curator's steps, with your verification ID and session already filled in; repeat with each curator's link.
